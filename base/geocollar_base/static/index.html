If a dog gets lost

When a dog has been outside its fences for 90 s, its collar asks for help. It shows up on Bluetooth as "Rex is lost 5551234567" (90 s on, 90 s off, to save battery) and every 5 minutes posts a plain text message on the public Meshtastic channel asking people to call you. It stops when the dog is back inside, or when you silence it.

Property lines

Import your parcel from your county's GIS or tax map site as GeoJSON, KML, or a shapefile (the .zip, or the .shp, .dbf and .prj files together). Click parcels on the map to select them, then make a keep-in zone from their outline, set back from the property line by as much as you like. Tax-map lines are often 1 to 5 m (3 to 16 ft) off, so check them against the satellite view.
    Set the perimeter back from the property line
    The collar shocks only in a zone's danger band, at most 2 times per escape and 4 per hour, never past the line, and never above level 30 of 99. Start low and try it on your own hand first.

      Simulation

      Simulated
      Not a real collar. It checks every position against its fences with the same rules as the collar firmware, so you can try fences before the hardware is ready.
      A real collar reports when its state changes, every 10 minutes, and every 30 s when lost. The simulator reports more often so you can watch it.

        Commands

        Collar firmware
        Get a firmware build, then flash it to a collar. The first install has to be over USB; after that a collar can be updated over Wi-Fi while it sits on its charger.

          Flash

          Updates the selected collar over your home Wi-Fi. The base asks the collar over the mesh to restart into Meshtastic's update loader, which joins the Wi-Fi network below and takes the new firmware. Put the collar on its charger within Wi-Fi range first: it does not enforce fences while it updates (usually 2 to 5 minutes).
